How a Genetic Algorithm works

  • Population: a group of candidate solutions. Here each one is a 5-bit binary chromosome, for example 10110.
  • Fitness: a score for how good a chromosome is. Here the fitness is x², where x is the decimal value, so 11111 (31) scores 961, the best possible.
  • Elitism (survival of the fittest): the best chromosome is copied unchanged into the next generation, so the best score is never lost.
  • Selection: fitter chromosomes are more likely to become parents (roulette wheel), just as stronger individuals pass on their genes more often.
  • Crossover (mating): two parents swap their tails at a random cut point, so each child mixes parts of both parents.
  • Mutation (adaptation): each bit flips with a small chance. This adds new variety that crossover alone cannot create, and it stops the population getting stuck.
  • New generation: the elite plus the mutated children replace the old population, and the cycle repeats.
  • Reading the graph: the blue line is the best fitness in each generation and the orange line is the average. Over time both rise toward 961 as the population converges on better solutions.
